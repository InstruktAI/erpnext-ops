# Business Relationships — Concept

## What

Relationship knowledge describes how the business works with a customer,
supplier, or other contact. It combines three distinct sources:

- The company's standing mandate: delegated activities, priorities, communication
  authority, and when its owner wants involvement or notice.
- Expressed preferences and agreements: contact person, channel, suitable timing,
  promised dates, and matters reserved for a personal conversation.
- Observed behavior: response patterns and relevant prior interactions, with
  their sources and uncertainty.

Company-wide rules belong in company knowledge and configuration. Party-specific
preferences and observations belong with the relevant ERP party/contact and its
activity trail, using the site's existing fields and records. Global procedures
describe how to use this knowledge; they do not contain individual preferences.

An observation such as a usual response interval informs judgment. It does not
become an agreement, a personality label, or authorization to contact someone.
Explicit corrections update the current understanding while the source history
remains available. Permission to use a class of communication comes from the
standing mandate and applicable policy, not a worker's impression of rapport.

The selected communication route is both appropriate to the relationship and
actually available. A recorded phone number, email address, or preferred channel
does not prove that the configured delivery surface can reach it. A communication
record is also distinct from evidence that an outbound message was delivered.

When a personal call or business decision belongs to the owner, the agent
prepares the missing information, consequence, prior attempts, and desired
contribution. The expectation remains open until evidence settles it, including
when responsibility for the next move belongs to the human.

## Why

The same missing response calls for different treatment across relationships.
Source-backed preferences help agents exercise delegated discretion without
repeatedly asking the owner or becoming intrusive. Keeping authority, preference,
and observation distinct allows learning without quietly changing the business's
commitments. A prepared handoff preserves the owner's time and relationship role.
